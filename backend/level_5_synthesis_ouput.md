## Stability and Bandgap of CsSnI3

Cesium tin iodide (CsSnI3) is a promising lead-free perovskite material for solar cell applications, offering a non-toxic alternative to lead-based counterparts [16, 22, 23]. However, its stability and bandgap characteristics exhibit variability depending on the specific phase and synthesis conditions.

Computational data from the Materials Project database indicates that CsSnI3 exists in multiple phases with differing stabilities. One phase (mp-aaabiyhx) is reported as stable with an energy above hull of 0.0 eV and a band gap of approximately 0.450 eV [1]. In contrast, other phases are identified as unstable, such as mp-aaaabond (energy above hull: 0.0032 eV, band gap: 2.063 eV) [2], mp-aaabgjcc (energy above hull: 0.0111 eV, band gap: 0.610 eV) [3], and mp-aaabjbuw (energy above hull: 0.0118 eV, band gap: 0.544 eV) [4]. This suggests that achieving stable CsSnI3 phases is crucial for practical applications. Literature also acknowledges "stability issues" in realizing defect-free device construction for CsSnI3 [5], although some studies highlight its "excellent thermal stability" as a beneficial property [6]. The conversion from unstable CsSnI3 to air-stable Cs2SnI6 has also been explored, indicating challenges with the intrinsic stability of CsSnI3 itself [7].

The bandgap of CsSnI3 is a critical parameter for its photovoltaic performance, and various values have been reported. Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations have identified CsSnI3 as a direct band gap semiconductor with a band gap of 0.95 eV [5]. Other studies suggest an ideal bandgap of 1.3 eV for Sn-based perovskites, including CsSnI3 [21, 22]. The Materials Project data further illustrates this variability, with bandgaps ranging from 0.450 eV to 2.063 eV across different phases [1, 2, 3, 4].

## Key Findings

Computational and experimental investigations over the last decade have provided deep insights into CsSnI3 perovskite solar cells:

*   **Computational Optimization:** Numerical simulations, primarily using the SCAPS-1D software, have been extensively employed to optimize the performance of CsSnI3-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) [16, 19, 22, 23, 24]. These studies focus on optimizing various device parameters, including the electron transport layer (ETL), hole transport layer (HTL), absorber layer thickness, doping density, and defect density [16, 19, 22, 23].
*   **Predicted Efficiencies:** Computational models have predicted high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) for optimized CsSnI3 PSCs. For instance, simulations have shown PCEs up to 23% for optimized CsSnI3 single crystals [8], 26.52% for CsSnI3/n-c-Si heterojunction solar cells [9], and 31.63% when using WS2 as an ETL [6]. An optimal device structure using SnO2 and Spiro-OMeTAD as ETL and HTL, respectively, achieved a simulated efficiency of 19.92% [10].
*   **Material Properties from DFT:** DFT calculations have been instrumental in evaluating the structural, optical, and electronic properties of the CsSnI3 perovskite absorber layer, confirming its direct band gap nature [5].
*   **Experimental Insights into Single Crystals:** Experimental work on melt-synthesized CsSnI3 ingots containing high-quality large single crystal (SC) grains has revealed superior photophysical properties. These include bulk carrier lifetimes reaching 6.6 ns, doping concentrations around 4.5 × 10^17 cm−3, and minority-carrier diffusion lengths approaching 1 µm. These values are significantly better than those observed in polycrystalline counterparts and are comparable to high-performance p-type photovoltaic materials [8]. CsSnI3 SCs also exhibit a very low surface recombination velocity of approximately 2 × 10^3 cm s−1, similar to lead-based perovskites [8].

## Conclusion

CsSnI3 holds significant promise as a lead-free alternative for perovskite solar cells, with both computational and experimental data highlighting its potential. While the material exhibits varying stability and bandgap values across different phases, computational studies have successfully identified optimal device architectures and predicted high power conversion efficiencies. Experimental advancements in synthesizing high-quality single crystals have demonstrated excellent intrinsic material properties, such as long minority-carrier diffusion lengths and low surface recombination velocities, which are crucial for high-performance photovoltaics. Overcoming the inherent stability challenges and precisely controlling the synthesis of desired CsSnI3 phases remain key areas for future research to fully realize its potential in cost-effective, high-efficiency, and non-toxic solar cells.
